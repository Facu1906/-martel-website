The Problem

The bottleneck is not the model.

It is that nobody wrote down how decisions actually get made.

Tribal knowledge lives in people's heads.

Processes live in spreadsheets nobody maintains.

Workflows exist only because someone once figured it out.

Getting that out is not a technical problem.

It is the first job. That is why we go in.

Why a Tool Cannot Do This

The expensive mistakes do not come from missing the obvious repetitive work. They come from removing the quiet stuff nobody documented.

Automation software ranks your work by cost and impact, then cuts what looks inefficient. The trouble is the informal checks holding a process together read as pure drag on that chart. Right up until they are gone and something breaks a month later.

We work inside long enough to see what the org chart does not. Then we automate what should be automated and leave what should not.
